CHART CHOICE JUSTIFICATION

"Which month had the highest total revenue?" (April = INR 419,417.43, May = INR 444,594.25, June = INR 398,055.24)

For this univariate temporal comparison, a vertical column chart is the optimal choice. It allows a regional manager to identify May as the peak month within a 10-second window. In accordance with chart-selection principles, the y-axis starts strictly at zero to prevent visual exaggeration of month-to-month variance, 3D effects are completely omitted to maintain data integrity, and a legend is unnecessary since the temporal categories are explicitly labeled on the horizontal axis.

"What percentage share does Ethnic Wear represent of April's total revenue?" (INR 104,520.77 of INR 419,417.43 = 24.92%)

For this univariate part-to-whole question, a single-series 100% proportional horizontal bar or a clean donut chart is best suited. This visualization immediately communicates the 24.92% share in under 10 seconds. The chart adheres to core principles by avoiding 3D distortion, anchoring values properly from a zero baseline, and omitting a legend entirely since the single data slice is labeled directly with its category and percentage.

"How do the four regions compare on total revenue?" (North/South/East/West figures from Part 1)

For this bivariate categorical comparison across the four geographic regions, a horizontal bar chart (regions on the y-axis, revenue on the x-axis) is ideal. Sorting the bars in descending order ensures the primary regional performance insight is grasped instantly within 10 seconds. The x-axis begins at zero to preserve accurate visual proportionality, 3D styling is avoided, and a legend is excluded because the discrete categories are clearly identified directly on the axis labels.
